## What it is

<!-- SF Symbol: app -->
Sift is a native macOS app that audits everything in /Applications: it explains
what each app is, scores how well it fits your workflow (1–5), tracks updates
across the App Store, Sparkle, and Homebrew, and keeps your license keys —
even for apps you've uninstalled. Analysis runs through local AI by default;
nothing about your machine leaves it unless you choose a cloud provider.

Built by **Veronica Loren** ([aka-kika](https://github.com/aka-kika)). The app I
use every day to decide what stays on my Mac.

## The milestone

<!-- SF Symbol: flag.checkered -->
First Developer ID-signed, Apple-notarized release — Sift now installs on any
Mac with a plain double-click, no Gatekeeper warnings. It is also the release
where AppAudit became Sift: same data, same keys, new name, and a month of
daily-use lessons turned into features.

## What shipped in 1.1.0

<!-- SF Symbol: shippingbox -->
- **Three AI providers** — local Ollama (default), plus Anthropic and OpenAI with
  fetched model lists; one shared prompt and parser across all of them.
- **No more surprise regeneration** — switching models keeps existing analyses;
  a banner offers a one-click re-analyze of just the drifted apps, and
  "Re-analyze All Apps" refreshes everything unlocked on demand.
- **License Vault** — keys for apps you've uninstalled stay retrievable
  (File → License Vault…, ⇧⌘L). Keys live in the Keychain, device-bound.
- **Last Used sort** — most-recently-used first, straight from Spotlight, with
  "Never used" apps sinking to the bottom. Subscription and Favorite tags too.
- **Recommendation-first detail panel** — score, best use, and reason on top; a
  single overflow menu instead of scattered buttons; CSV export of the full
  audit (never license keys).

## The story (how 1.1.0 came together)

<!-- SF Symbol: book.closed -->
- Born as AppAudit in a single day (May 12) — scanner, local AI analysis, and a
  route-aware update assistant, built because no app on the Mac could answer
  "do I actually need this?"
- A month of daily use exposed the real problems: hedgy "appears to be" analyses
  (the prompt literally instructed the model to hedge), silent re-analysis
  wipes on model switches, and an ad-hoc test build that prompted for the
  Keychain on every license-key read. Each became a fix: a de-hedged prompt, the
  drift banner, and bundle-ID-derived isolation for side-builds.
- One long June day turned feedback into features — subscription markers, Last
  Used sort, License Vault, CSV export, the recommendation-first detail panel,
  cloud providers — and ended with a rename: AppAudit became Sift, keeping its
  bundle identity so eight purchased license keys carried over untouched.
- The final stretch had a twist: the macOS 27 golden beta broke the standalone
  Command Line Tools mid-release. The freshly installed Xcode 27 beta toolchain
  rescued the build, the packaging script learned its new product layout, and
  notarization came back **Accepted** on the first submission.
