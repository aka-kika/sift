## What it is

<!-- SF Symbol: app -->
Sift is a native macOS app that audits everything in /Applications: it explains
what each app is, scores how well it fits your workflow, tracks updates, and
keeps your license keys — now behind Touch ID, with the account each license
belongs to. Analysis runs through local AI by default and grounds itself in
evidence: the app's own metadata, its website's own words, and what you actually
use.

Built by **Veronica Loren** ([aka-kika](https://github.com/aka-kika)). The app I
use every day to decide what stays on my Mac.

## The milestone

<!-- SF Symbol: flag.checkered -->
The grounding release. 1.2.0 turns analysis from plausible prose into
evidence-first reporting: facts before opinions in the panel, a workflow profile
derived automatically from the apps you actually have and use, app categories as
visible metadata, and saved links that are *fetched* — so the model reads what a
site says instead of guessing from its domain name. It is also the release where
Sift went native on macOS 26/27 with Liquid Glass, gained Apple Intelligence as
a zero-setup engine, locked license keys behind Touch ID, and got its gold
magnifier icon.

## What shipped in 1.2.0

<!-- SF Symbol: shippingbox -->
- **Grounded-first analysis** — "What is this?" leads the panel; the relevance
  verdict follows. The workflow profile builds itself from your installed apps
  (categories, recently used, open right now) with an optional custom override.
- **Links with receipts** — a saved app link is fetched (title + description)
  and quoted to the model as primary evidence; domain names are explicitly
  demoted to weak hints.
- **Liquid Glass on macOS 26/27** — glass-prominent actions, scroll-edge fades,
  grouped toolbar; classic appearance preserved on macOS 14–15.
- **Apple Intelligence engine** — on-device Foundation Models with few-shot
  tuned instructions, user style notes, a truthful Private Cloud Compute status
  probe, and zero-setup default for new installs on capable Macs.
- **License keys, properly** — Touch ID (or password) on every copy or reveal;
  the Vault is now the complete key collection, sectioned Installed / No longer
  installed, with cached app icons and the email each license is registered to.
- **Honesty in the UI** — when an analysis is weak and unlinked, Sift says so
  and offers Add Link right in the panel.
- **New app icon** — a gold magnifier over a glass app grid.

## The story (how 1.2.0 came together)

<!-- SF Symbol: book.closed -->
- The macOS 27 golden beta landed mid-cycle and broke the standalone Command
  Line Tools; the freshly installed Xcode 27 beta toolchain carried every build
  from then on.
- Apple Intelligence came back, went away, and came back again: an empirical lab
  proved Private Cloud Compute silently fails on this beta while reporting
  itself available — so Sift now probes it honestly and tells the truth in
  Settings. Few-shot instructions and seeded sampling tamed the on-device
  model's "Use it…" monotony.
- A Mac cleaner that lives at `mole.fit` got analyzed as a fitness tracker —
  the smoking gun that URL strings were masquerading as evidence. Links are
  fetched now; the page's own words ground the analysis.
- The owner's "1 more thing" cascade gave the license story its final shape:
  Touch ID on every reveal, the Vault as a complete sectioned collection with
  cached icons, and per-key registered emails for licenses bought under
  different accounts.
- The icon came from a MiniMax render: Vision's subject-lift pulled the artwork
  free, a measured squircle rebuilt the plate, and the magnifier handle kept its
  classic overhang.
